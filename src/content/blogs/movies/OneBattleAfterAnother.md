Maybe it’s because I’m not entirely dialed into the U.S. political landscape, but this movie just didn’t hit the mark for me, neither emotionally nor as pure entertainment.

![One Battle After Another ](../../../assets/blog/movies/OneBattleAfterAnother.jpg "medium")

## Story

The story was… fine? Most character arcs got wrapped up reasonably well, though the ending left me with a lukewarm *"Hm, ok."*

The "good guys" win, I guess. Colonel Lockjaw gets his very deserved dirt nap, and Leo reunites with his daughter—who finally decides to call him "Dad" instead of "Bob" by the end credits. Wholesome! Great!

But honestly? I was left wondering what it was all for. As far as the actual resistance goes, zero real progress was made. They basically just hit reset and returned to the status quo.

I *get* what PTA was probably going for here—the whole endless cycle of revolutions, passing the torch, and the idea that the fight never truly ends in America (hence the title, plus Willa getting called off to another mission at the very end). But if that was the main message, it felt pretty diluted.

We had *so* many subplots crammed into this runtime:

* The whole "who’s Willa’s real dad?" soap opera.
* Bob spending 90% of the movie playing lost-and-found with Willa.
* The "Big Bad Boys Club" of ultra-wealthy, super-powerful, definitely-racist dudes.
* Street riots.
* Immigrants locked in cages.

It all made the film feel a bit like a messy buffet—lots of options, but no clear main dish. Definitely a case of breadth over depth.

While I respect the movie calling out real-world political horrors and the grim reality of military/government overreach, PTA feels more like a passive observer than someone actually taking a stand. Which is fine! But paired with the chaotic subplots, it just felt unfocused.

Worse, the tonal shifts gave me severe emotional whiplash:

1. **Act I:** Heavy, brutal, serious. A literal raid on an immigration camp, bombings, extreme violence, and Perfidia snitching on the entire French 75.
2. **Time Jump:** Suddenly we’re in a cozy family drama about Bob and Willa’s trust issues.
3. **Mid-game:** High-stakes drama morphs into a semi-goofy comedy as Bob tries to put his "revolutionary" hat back on with Sergio’s help.
4. **Finale:** Back to grim reality with Willa escaping the military and the big family reunion.

Just a bit all over the place for me.

## Pacing

It felt *long*. The movie spends almost the entire runtime teasing this massive, epic showdown between Colonel Lockjaw and Bob Ferguson... and then they don't even really face off.

To make matters worse, Bob doesn't even save Willa! She basically rescues herself, making Bob's whole quest feel like a cinematic wild goose chase.

## Acting

Zero complaints here—the stacked cast carried this movie on their backs. Every actor gave their character a distinct personality, and honestly, the performances were the best part of the whole experience.

The dynamic between Bob and Sergio was a highlight, and Sean Penn was, well... Sean Penn doing what he does best.

It was the few things that I enjoyed from the movie and there was some individual scenes that were my highlight. For example, when Deandra from the resistance had to extract Willa from the school dance and had to quickly convince Willa to fully trust her and escape. It was a great scene!

## Overall

Technically speaking? It’s stellar. The cinematography, the score, the acting—it’s all top-tier work from a seasoned director.

Where it stumbles is the narrative and pacing. It felt like a movie going through an identity crisis, unsure of what it actually wanted to be when it grew up. It still lands its main punches about government cruelty, but overall, it’s a stylish, winding road that left me a bit numb.
